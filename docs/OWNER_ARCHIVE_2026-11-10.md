# Owner business archive

The primary owner can request a fixed snapshot from More → Business Archive, prepare it in bounded parts, and download every ZIP with its SHA-256 manifest. All plans retain access; creating, preparing and downloading does not call Fin or reserve an AI allowance.

Version 1 has explicit columns across 84 business/owner collections, documented in `shared/archiveCatalog.ts`. It includes original invoice, estimate and change-order records/items, payments/refunds, expenses, customers/properties, jobs/visits/tasks/time/materials/costs/completion snapshots, worker profiles/availability, recurring plans/history, consent/preferences, customer-visible support messages, owner Fin conversations and configuration metadata. Every stored object in the business prefix of the photo, logo and immutable-document-PDF buckets is included. Passwords, provider credentials, authorization-token fields, internal support notes and other users' private conversations are excluded.

The database copies all selected records in one statement. The snapshot is immutable even if the business later edits records. Missing attachments stop archive creation; changed file metadata or bytes stop preparation. An archive is never called ready while a part is incomplete. If source files change before copying finishes, request a fresh snapshot.

Records are UTF-8 JSON Lines grouped into bounded ZIP parts. Stored files are grouped into parts of at most 20 files and 18 MB of source bytes. Part 1 includes the field catalog. Each part has its own manifest with the archive ID, snapshot time, full collection counts, expected number of parts and file hashes. Keep all parts and extract them into one folder; record filenames include part numbers and do not overwrite one another. This is an owner record archive, not an automatic database restore/import.

Requests use an owner-generated UUID for retry identity. One active preparation lease prevents overlapping work; an uncertain upload can be retried after two minutes. Final parts are checked by SHA-256 both before serving and in the browser. Downloads require a fresh signed-in primary-owner check; the private archive bucket denies direct client access and has no customer-share URL. Loss of ownership immediately removes access.

Downloads expire seven days after the request. The existing authenticated five-minute worker removes only generated archive objects and snapshots after a twenty-minute expiry buffer; original business records and source buckets are untouched. Cleanup still runs when sending is disabled. A failed object deletion leaves metadata available for another cleanup attempt. No inactive-account deletion is enabled.

Resource bounds: two snapshots per business per rolling day; 250 MB of snapshot JSON per request; a single record at most 10 MB; ZIP parts at most 24 MB; source files follow the existing 10 MB upload bound; cleanup at most 100 parts per invocation. Larger unsupported exports fail explicitly rather than truncate. The 50,001-customer SQL test proves there is no workbook-style 50,000-row cutoff. High-volume hosted capacity and cost acceptance remain separate gates.

Local validation: build/startup checks; synthetic PostgreSQL access, immutability, 50,001 records, leases, seven-day expiry and non-destructive cleanup; independent Python ZIP reader/CRC verification; source/path/size checks; revoked access, corrupt downloads and lost-upload handling. CI additionally replays the full application schema with genuine local Auth sessions and private Storage uploads/downloads, and runs desktop/phone browser retry/pause/download journeys.
