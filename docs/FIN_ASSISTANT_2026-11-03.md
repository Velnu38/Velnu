# Fin: business questions and natural-language document review

The office assistant is named **Fin**. The owner can update Fin's saved business knowledge through Settings → Company profile: business description, services, area and team responsibilities. Fin reads that profile and current permitted records at question time; it does not train or fine-tune a model, crawl other businesses, or autonomously remember unverified claims.

The `fin-assistant` Edge Function has two read-only modes. `draft` extracts separate descriptions, quantities and stated amounts into a reviewed invoice or estimate. `question` explains a bounded snapshot of this business's saved profile, customers, unpaid invoices, estimates, active jobs, Finviant schedule and reminders. Answers show supporting record labels, check time and retrieval limits. Business questions are initially limited to owner/admin; draft interpretation additionally permits office staff. Database RLS and explicit business/user filters apply to every read through the caller's JWT. No service-role read client, arbitrary SQL, model-selected table or write tool is provided.

Each question snapshot includes up to 50 records per category. Customers and estimates are newest-first; unpaid invoices, active jobs and reminders are ordered by date, and the schedule overlaps yesterday through the next 31 days. Category counts and partial-result warnings are supplied to the model and shown in the UI. Paid invoice history, external email/calendar events, bank balances, expense reporting and complete historical search are outside this initial answer scope. Retrieved invoice sums are calculated in exact decimal cents, with draft balances separated from issued debts. This is not an account-wide balance when the set is truncated. Profile descriptions and record text remain untrusted reference data.

Every AI-generated draft line includes an exact quotation from the request. Server and browser validation reject unsupported amounts, monetary customer names, invented customers, ungrounded tax/total values, invalid ranges and unsafe payment/negative/discount instructions. A missing customer leaves the customer selector empty while preserving charges and showing the total. Saving remains the established reviewed, atomic, retry-safe `create_document` operation. There is no automatic send or charge.

The deterministic fallback also fixes the reported instruction: “Create invoice for $200 for landscaping, $100 for painting.” It returns landscaping $200, painting $100, no customer, and a $300 calculated total. The owner does not have to dictate a total. The draft uses compact Description/Amount rows side by side on phones, Add line, optional units/tax/date controls, and a total at the bottom. Review hides the original entry controls to reduce scrolling. Unsupported or ambiguous text stays editable and unpriced rather than guessed.

A new key was provisioned securely into the user-approved, ignored `.env.local`. Three live Responses API checks using synthetic business data passed: the reported two-charge request, an estimate with the customer named at the end, and a profile question. Live results passed the draft provenance validator. No real customer information or production business mutation was involved. Live AI output is probabilistic; these examples do not prove all dictation patterns.

Server-secret configuration still requires an authenticated Supabase dashboard session. The installed Supabase connector can deploy functions but does not expose a secret-setting operation; the local CLI has no management login. The key must be provisioned as server-only `OPENAI_API_KEY` in staging, never as a VITE variable or a repository value. Until that step is verified, the app retains deterministic draft review and clearly reports unavailable business answers. Actual mobile/noisy-audio transcription and a signed-in business-answer walkthrough remain live verification steps.
