### Requests to Fin and additional purchases

Use the customer-facing phrase “requests to Fin,” not credits, tokens or searches. One successfully completed user task uses one request; internal provider calls, lookups, clarification steps and automatic retries do not create extra customer charges. Failed tasks release the reserved request. Manual document creation, sending, export, customer approvals, payments and resulting status changes do not consume Fin requests. Free introductory use is limited to document drafting; paid plans receive their designed assistant capabilities.

Enforce a bounded heavy-request workload and track tokens/provider costs internally. Tell the user upfront when a task exceeds supported scope instead of silently charging multiple requests. Reserve usage atomically, settle once on success and prevent duplicate charges across retries and concurrent tabs. Paid monthly included allowances reset on the billing cycle and do not roll over.

Offer paid plans explicitly purchased packs of 100 additional requests to Fin for $6.99 or 250 for $15.99, with a separate purchased balance. No automatic overage purchase. Disclose the final pack expiry/refund terms before checkout; these terms still need to be finalized. A request pack does not extend an account's subscription, Free period or record-retention deadline.

### Inactive and expired accounts — recommended retention policy

Use paid entitlement and a fixed expiry date to control storage retention. A login, background sync, email open, customer-link visit or automated payment callback must not keep an unpaid workspace in storage indefinitely.

| Account or workspace state | Access and recommended retention |
| --- | --- |
| Active paid Individual or Business | Keep records within the subscribed storage limit while payment is current. After 180 days without an owner sign-in, send a service reminder; do not delete data or cancel a paying subscription solely for inactivity. |
| Active 15-day trial or active 90-day Free period | Honor the advertised term even if the owner has not signed in. Show the exact end date and subsequent deletion date. |
| Trial ends with no plan selection | Stop creation/sending and paid automation. Allow owner view/download/export for 60 days from trial end, then delete the workspace's records. A first eligible Free activation or verified paid activation before deletion can restore entitled access. |
| Free reaches its 90-day end | Stop creation/sending and paid automation. Allow owner view/download/export for 60 days, then delete the workspace's records unless paid access is activated. No second Free period. |
| Paid subscription cancelled or not renewed | Retain normal access through the paid-through date. At entitlement end, allow 60 days of owner view/download/export, then delete unless paid access resumes or an unused eligible Free period is explicitly selected. |
| Failed subscription payment | Recommend one fixed 7-day payment-recovery grace period after failed renewal, with notices and no duplicate billing. Do not refill paid Fin or managed-delivery allowances until renewal payment is verified. If unpaid at its end, begin the same 60-day read-only retention period. Repeated retries or sign-ins cannot restart it. |
| Unverified signup or account that never activates a workspace | Remove abandoned signup/onboarding data after 30 days from signup, with notices 7 days and 1 day before deletion. Exclude any account with a paid, active, beta-exempt or other retained workspace membership. |

The 60-day window is the full owner recovery/export window; do not add an indefinite archive afterward. Use server-generated UTC deadlines, display the owner's local date/time and retain policy-version acceptance. A trial immediately followed by the 90-day Free period provides up to 105 days of active use, then 60 days to export: records are deleted on day 165 if no paid activation occurs. Ordinary backup copies must then age out within the separately disclosed backup window.

Send a clear in-app notice and transactional email when paid/free access ends, then deletion warnings 30 days, 7 days and 1 day before the scheduled purge. Each notice must state the exact date, affected business, what will be lost, the export link and the plan activation link. Do not use marketing opt-out settings to suppress necessary account-service notices. Track delivery/retries and expose failures to the administrator; a bounced email must not silently become an unlimited retention extension. Show both upcoming deletions and failed cleanup jobs in the Admin Dashboard.

During read-only retention, existing Finviant-hosted customer approval and payment links may continue within their original document terms; persist and export resulting financial/status changes. Stop new paid outbound automation when entitlement ends. At deletion, revoke hosted document, scheduling and E-Business Card links and return a professional unavailable message. Tell owners in advance that these links end with deletion. Direct payment-provider URLs and previously downloaded/emailed files are outside Finviant's complete control; do not promise to erase customer-held copies or reverse settled payments. Reconcile pending provider events before purging and retain only the narrowly required transaction evidence under a documented hold.

Before automatic deletion is enabled, provide a complete downloadable owner archive: customers and addresses; estimates, invoices and change orders with their final PDFs; jobs; approval/payment history and balances; receipts, photos and attachments; and worker/availability records. Include a manifest and counts, test large exports, and protect downloads with owner authorization and short-lived links. An export must not consume Fin requests or require a subscription. Temporary archive downloads should expire and be removed after 7 days, never extending past workspace deletion. Existing partial exports are not adequate evidence that this requirement is complete.

Delete the tenant's database records, stored objects and versions, PDFs, photos, receipts, transcripts, generated archives, cached copies and search/AI-derived records; revoke OAuth credentials, public tokens and queued jobs. Use supported storage deletion so objects do not remain billable after database rows disappear. Remove tenant memberships and delete an Auth identity only if it has no other active/retained workspace. Never purge another tenant, the operating administrator or existing exempt beta workspaces. Delete orphaned uploads separately and bound temporary files so storage usage cannot grow between cleanup runs.

Backups need a separate, verified limit: recommend ordinary backup copies age out within 30 additional days after live-data deletion, with no customer restore after purge. Confirm every selected provider's actual retention and contractual constraints before promising that maximum. Database backups do not include Supabase Storage objects, so inventory and expire file backups separately. A disaster restore must reapply a minimal deletion ledger before reopening the service to prevent deleted records returning. Restrict the ledger to non-content identifiers and policy/job evidence, expire it after all applicable backup windows and any documented legal requirement, and never log raw documents or credentials.

“All records deleted” applies to the business workspace's service data, subject to specific legal holds. Finviant's own subscription receipts/tax records and narrowly required security, fraud or dispute evidence may need separate statutory retention; retain the minimum, restrict access, set a documented end date and review active holds quarterly. Do not retain a whole former customer's workspace indefinitely as a billing exception. Owners must download their own financial/tax records before deletion. The IRS describes different retention periods by circumstance; Finviant's 60-day service window is not a substitute for the owner's recordkeeping duties. The FTC recommends a written policy covering what is kept, why, how long and secure disposal. Review the final customer terms/privacy notice before rollout and apply the new policy to existing public users only after clear prospective notice. Do not retroactively expire the private-beta grants.

### Independent recommendation and commercial positioning

Prioritize advanced job operations and useful downloads. A narrowly scoped field-report app is a plausible second subscription product after demand is demonstrated. Full payroll is a poor near-term expansion for this small operation: provider agreements, payroll errors, payment timing and customer support add substantial responsibilities. Payroll preparation/export is a more manageable adjacent capability. Basic recurring invoices are common enough that they should strengthen the existing plans; they are a weak standalone paid upgrade.

These are positioning hypotheses, not proven market gaps. Bookipi advertises AI tools, a scheduler and recurring invoices. Invoice2go offers appointment scheduling; Jobber advertises job costing, progress billing and recurring work. Wave already sells US payroll at $40/month plus $6 per active employee or contractor paid; Gusto's Simple retail plan is $49/month plus $6 per person. Those retail prices are competitor benchmarks, not wholesale quotes available to Finviant. Value must come from the particular workflow, ease of use and total price a customer can justify.

Basic onboarding and self-service customer-list import should be included within plan limits when available. A standard $79 import fee is withdrawn from these recommendations. Substantial manual cleanup or historical migration can be individually quoted after inspecting scope, without making ordinary setup a paid obstacle. Preserve the complete standard estimate-to-payment Business workflow when pricing new optional capabilities.

### How it connects to the current workflow

1. A directly created job or an approved estimate resolves to the correct existing job once. Estimate revisions remain linked; approval does not create duplicate jobs on retry.
2. Scheduling creates or changes the appropriate visit/appointment through the current backend validation. Customer booking rechecks availability at commit. Crew assignment uses worker profiles, hours, time off and overlapping work; external-calendar failures show a degraded state rather than claiming conflict-free scheduling.
3. Work starts; tasks, photos, materials and time entries attach to that job/visit. Changes refresh Jobs, the main calendar, Home and the relevant customer snapshot through the existing business refresh mechanism without remounting editors.
4. Owner/authorized staff confirms completion after required work is resolved. Finviant prepares or locates the correct invoice for review, including previous deposits/progress allocations and approved changes. Nothing is emailed merely because a worker checks a box or a job changes stage.
5. Verified customer payment updates invoice balance, financial reporting and applicable attention items. Job completed and invoice paid remain independent facts. Refunds/credits update the financial side without rewriting the operational history.
6. Reopening, a callback or an additional visit preserves the prior invoice, completion evidence and cost history. Unpaid invoices and unresolved work must remain visible after a job is archived.

### Profitability: build a real job-cost view

The current Financial Performance totals are income, expenses and Net Cash Flow. Those are useful and should remain intact. They do not establish the profit earned on an individual job. A professional job-cost feature should present these quantities separately:

| --- | --- |

The current finance aggregation excludes non-USD rows; do not silently combine currencies or turn read-only external accounting data into a second authoritative ledger. A 50% operating-margin floor permits $14 total operating cost, leaving approximately $1.57/account/month modeled headroom for extra load. This is narrow, unmeasured headroom, not an approved module budget or guaranteed profit; the model does not establish a 50% net margin after development, acquisition, support labor and taxes. Measure and allocate those costs before pricing a promise.

Keep costs low with start/stop timer events, server summaries, bounded recurring-work generation, in-app notifications, image compression, shared storage/message quotas and no routine AI call. Use email digests for many routine worker changes rather than delivering one email/SMS per task update. Managed recipient deliveries and SMS segments count across customer and internal messages. Do not silently reduce current included functionality to fund the new module or add unlimited photos/video, route APIs or AI searches.

Include the standard Jobs core in Business if measured unit economics meet the owner's minimum margin requirement. Preserve baseline job handling on lower tiers; use underlined links to explain Business-only depth. Consider separately priced advanced services only when there is demonstrated demand and measured costs. Full payroll, continuous GPS/geofencing, route optimization/traffic APIs, warehouse procurement, regulated certificates, heavy/unlimited media and unrestricted field collaboration remain outside the low-cost baseline unless separately scoped. These exclusions mean Finviant must not claim universal enterprise parity.

### Competitor benchmark and differentiating value

Jobber documents recurring/multi-visit work, dispatch, job checklists, time/expense tracking and job profitability; Housecall Pro documents job scheduling, mobile work, time tracking and recurring-job workflows. ServiceM8 offers a broad service-job workflow and currently advertises a US $29/month Starter plan with unlimited users and 50 jobs/month. Its higher-level job-costing/resource/form capabilities have their own plan conditions; do not attribute every advanced feature to Starter. These products set a genuine operational benchmark. AI, scheduling and recurring jobs are not unique in this market.

Finviant's proposed differentiator should be an understandable approved-scope-to-payment workflow, clear job margin with missing-cost warnings, simple mobile actions, professional branded documents, reusable work templates and integrated Fin assistance at a sustainable small-business price. Compare effective jobs/users/storage/messages/features, device support and reliability, not only a competitor's highest advertised price. Finviant's 25 profiles must not be compared with 25 competitor login users. The competitive claim becomes defensible only after the same scenarios are tested in Finviant and the selected peer plan.

## Capacity plan and acceptance targets

Treat hundreds of registered businesses, simultaneous logged-in people and public customer-link traffic as different loads. Start with an isolated production-like dataset of at least **300 workspaces**, separate authenticated tenant sessions, realistic histories/files and skewed heavy accounts. Ramp **10 → 50 → 100 active users**, record achieved requests/second and transaction mix, run a **60-minute soak**, then test a controlled spike and queue recovery. Increase toward 1,000 workspaces only after measured acceptance. The old 1,000 input-profile campaign is not equivalent to 1,000 customer accounts.

Proposed starting targets, to approve and measure: ordinary read/write API p95 ≤1 second and <1% unexpected failures; no cross-tenant data, duplicate financial mutations or duplicate confirmed sends; p75 LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1 on representative mobile journeys; 99.9% monthly service availability as an operational goal, not a current SLA. Report PDF/OCR/voice latency separately from ordinary API calls. Start alerts around 70–80% sustained resource/connection pressure, any dead-letter job, and queue lag beyond two normal schedule intervals.

Proposed recovery goals: RPO ≤15 minutes and RTO ≤4 hours if the selected backup/PITR and storage plan can demonstrably achieve them. Otherwise document achievable numbers and make an explicit business decision. Do not promise those goals before a restore drill.

Bound tests to approved staging infrastructure and provider policies. Stub billable third-party sends/AI/payment capture at scale; run a few separately authorized live-provider checks. Do not load-test the current beta domain or create hundreds of persistent customer accounts during an assessment.

Suggested rollout: 10 real pilot businesses → 50 → 100 → 300, with an owner-reviewed 48–72-hour stability/incident/usage window at each step. Gate expansion on monitored error rates, support load, provider success, queue lag and actual capacity; these windows are recommendations, not measured history.

## How this report stays current

- Canonical task and evidence state: `docs/launch-readiness.json`. `npm run assessment:sync` deterministically regenerates this report; `npm run assessment:check` rejects stale output and unsupported completion records.

- A verified completion is recorded with `npm run assessment:complete -- ID --evidence "receipt/test/provider verification reference"`. It requires a date and immutable code commit, removes that item from the report, and preserves its evidence in the registry. Source existence or mocked tests alone must not close an external-provider, capacity, restore or real-device gate. Reopen regressions with `npm run assessment:reopen -- ID --reason "failure evidence"`.

- `.github/workflows/assessment-report.yml` validates PRs and regenerates on trusted release-branch pushes. If only the report is stale, the trusted job writes a report-only commit using a non-forced, exact-parent Git update. No credentials or customer data belong in the report.

- A separate daily evidence review checks for newly verified completions, regressions and provider/configuration changes. It updates the registry/report only with adequate evidence. This is a periodic check, not an instantaneous subscription to every external account setting.

- Project `AGENTS.md` requires readiness updates as part of future work. The old campaign publisher cannot overwrite this current launch assessment. Historical campaigns and completed fixes remain in Git history and existing evidence files, rather than recurring as unfinished work.

- A remote CI pass, deployment READY, scheduler dispatch, real-provider success and real-phone acceptance are distinct evidence types. Keep unknown/unavailable checks open and describe unavailable access rather than guessing.
